One desk cannot tend a library this size.

Knowledge commons like the Internet Archive hold more projects than any single office can garden alone. Meta-curation is how the tending gets shared. This page describes the role, and how I practice it.

For years, one desk decided what got described, checked, and surfaced across a commons' many unmanaged projects. That desk did careful work, but the collection kept growing past what any one office could reach. Requests queued. Whole corners went untended, not from neglect, but from scale.

Meta-curation is a change of shape, not a change of ambition: the same care, held by many hands instead of one. A meta-curator tends the gardeners, so the gardeners can tend the commons.

In the commons, local experts propose, describe and vouch for the projects they already know. Shared conventions, not a shared queue, keep everyone legible to everyone else. The lighthouse still coordinates, but it stops being the only hand on every file.

What a meta-curator does

  • Convenes the people doing the work, so managers do not have to convene at all. The builders meet; the decisions travel with them.
  • Resumes stalled conversations between projects that need each other but drifted apart, and stays with them until a concrete roadmap names which person from which project comes together.
  • Maps overlaps: where two organizations' aspirations already point at the same missing piece, and neither has noticed yet.
  • Keeps everyone legible: shared conventions, public briefs, monthly written updates and milestone-based check-ins instead of standing meetings.
  • Weaves resources: connects the projects being coordinated with funders who match them, so the coordination also feeds the work it reveals.

Hosted by many, owned by none

A meta-curator earns trust from many stakeholders by belonging exclusively to none of them. The role grew in conversation with the Internet Archive, and it works for the Archive. It does not depend on it.

Any organization that benefits from tended commons can host or fund a share of the role: archives, privacy projects, foundations, public programs, municipalities. A stable base retained by several hands keeps the curator independent, and independence is what makes the coordination trustworthy to every side of the table.
